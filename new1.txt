\begin{abstract}
Wireless sensor networks in smart energy systems must manage mixed-criticality traffic under rigid battery constraints, prioritizing urgent fault alerts over routine telemetry. This work examines the efficacy of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for packet scheduling within an ns-3 and OpenAI Gym framework. We train a Double DQN scheduler with prioritized replay and a state-aware reward function designed to avert priority queue starvation. Over 20 matched, seed-controlled simulations, our learned controller is benchmarked against Strict Priority and Random schedulers. While the DRL agent vastly improves upon random allocation and maintains a 95.78\% high-priority delivery rate, it does not surpass the Strict Priority heuristic on raw HP-latency. However, the agent learns a valuable trade-off, reducing normal telemetry delay by 30.5\% by dynamically balancing buffer loads. We present these results as a reproducible benchmark defining the operational trade-offs for learning-based schedulers in smart grid communications.
\keywords{Smart Energy Systems \and Wireless Sensor Networks \and Reinforcement Learning \and ns-3 \and QoS \and Priority Scheduling}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\vspace{-2mm}
Modern power grids depend on edge-tier Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) to monitor substations, track distribution feeders, and signal electrical faults. Nodes in these environments face harsh channel dynamics and stringent energy constraints while handling mixed-criticality traffic. Critical alarm bursts (e.g., breaker trip commands) must be delivered with minimal latency, while routine telemetry (e.g., load profiling) requires reliable eventual delivery to maintain grid observability \cite{frikha2021wrlsurvey,leong2020automatica}.

When traffic surges congest local gateways, the scheduling discipline dictates network-wide performance. Traditional deployments use rule-based heuristics like Strict Priority (SP) for their simplicity and deterministic behavior. SP excels at delivering high-priority (HP) traffic by unconditionally serving the HP queue first. However, during extended grid disturbances with sustained alarm bursts, SP completely starves the normal-priority (NP) queue, leading to massive buffer overflows and a "state estimation blackout" at the control center.

Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) offers an adaptive solution by framing scheduling as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), allowing gateways to learn dynamic policies from environmental feedback \cite{luong2019drlsurvey}. However, model-free DRL is prone to training instability and policy collapse. A poorly shaped reward function can easily steer an agent toward degenerate local optima, such as ignoring HP traffic entirely to maximize the delivery of more frequent NP packets.

This paper provides a rigorous empirical evaluation of a policy-guided Double Deep Q-Network (Double-DQN) scheduler using a co-simulation framework linking PyTorch with the ns-3 network simulator \cite{ns3gym,boni2021ns3gym}. We investigate a focused systems question: can a learned DRL policy maintain robust HP service while offering a stable and beneficial performance trade-off compared to the SP baseline under fair, seed-synchronized evaluation conditions? Our contributions are: 1) A reproducible ns-3/PyTorch pipeline with run-level artifact verification; 2) A soft policy-guidance reward function to prevent policy collapse; 3) A statistically significant comparison against baselines over 20 matched runs; and 4) A transparent characterization of the fairness-latency trade-off, where the DRL agent reduces NP latency by 30.5\% while sustaining a 95.78\% HP delivery ratio.

\section{System Model and Problem Formulation}\vspace{-2mm}
\subsection{Network and Traffic Model}
We model a smart grid sensing cluster of 100 wireless nodes in a 150\,m $\times$ 150\,m area around a central gateway, simulated in ns-3.40. The network uses the IEEE 802.15.4 MAC/PHY standard. Nodes generate two traffic classes: high-priority (HP) fault alerts (bursty Poisson process, $\lambda_H = 0.2$ packets/s) and normal-priority (NP) telemetry (periodic, $T_N = 2.0$\,s). Each node has separate FIFO buffers ($B_H=50, B_N=100$) with distinct timeout deadlines ($T_H^{\text{max}}=1.0$\,s, $T_N^{\text{max}}=3.0$\,s). The DRL agent at the gateway makes scheduling decisions every 100\,ms.

\subsection{MDP Definition}
The scheduling task is a discounted MDP with a 6-dimensional normalized state vector $\mathbf{s}_t = [q_t^{N}, q_t^{H}, e_t, a_t^{N}, a_t^{H}, d_t]$, representing NP/HP queue occupancy, residual energy, NP/HP packet age, and node density. The discrete action space is $\mathcal{A} = \{\text{send HP (0)}, \text{send normal (1)}, \text{drop normal (2)}, \text{sleep (3)}, \text{drop HP (4)}\}$.

\subsection{Reward Formulation with Soft Policy Guidance}
To prevent policy collapse while maintaining flexibility, we use a bounded, soft-guided reward function. The total reward $r_t = \tanh((r_t^{\text{base}} + r_t^{\text{guide}})/100)$ combines a baseline component for core performance (delivery, drops, latency, energy) with a state-aware guidance term. The guidance term $r_t^{\text{guide}}$ provides a bonus ($\alpha=6.0$) for serving the HP queue and a penalty ($-\beta$, where $\beta=8.0$) for ignoring it only when the oldest HP packet's age $a_t^H$ exceeds an urgency threshold $\tau_u=0.55$. This nudges the agent to prioritize aging HP packets without forcing it to mimic SP, allowing it to learn more nuanced, balanced policies.

\section{Methodology}\vspace{-2mm}
\subsection{Learning Architecture and Baselines}
We use a Double-DQN agent with Prioritized Experience Replay (PER) to mitigate Q-value overestimation and improve sample efficiency. The architecture is a standard MLP with two 64-neuron hidden layers. Training uses an $\epsilon$-greedy exploration strategy, with $\epsilon$ decaying from 1.0 to 0.05. We compare against two baselines: **Strict Priority (SP)**, a deterministic heuristic, and **Random** selection.

\subsection{Training and Evaluation Protocol}
The model is trained for a maximum of 50 epochs, with early stopping after 15 epochs of no improvement in the validation score. The validation score is a composite metric of PDR, PLR, and HP delivery metrics, designed to penalize HP starvation. The best-performing checkpoint (achieved at Epoch 12) was used for final evaluation. The final benchmarking consists of 20 independent runs for each policy, using identical, matched random seeds to ensure a fair comparison. Statistical significance is determined using paired $t$-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests based on normality checks (Shapiro-Wilk), with a significance level of $\alpha=0.05$.

\subsection{Comparative Performance Analysis}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} summarizes the final performance metrics. The policy-guided DQN agent dramatically outperforms the Random baseline on all metrics, confirming that it learned a coherent scheduling strategy. For example, it boosts throughput by 151\% and cuts energy use by 45\%.

Against the specialized Strict Priority heuristic, the DRL agent does not achieve superior HP-centric performance. SP delivers higher throughput and a lower HP latency (0.0274s vs. 0.1431s). However, the DQN agent's key contribution lies in its ability to balance the needs of both traffic classes. It reduces the average latency of normal-priority packets by **30.5\%** (from 0.6171s to 0.4287s), a statistically significant improvement ($p < .001$) with a large effect size ($d_z = -3.3284$). This demonstrates that the agent successfully learned to prevent the starvation of normal telemetry traffic---a critical flaw of the greedy SP approach. The latency CDFs in Figure~\ref{fig:plots} visualize this trade-off: SP's NP latency curve has a long tail, while the DQN agent's curve is shifted significantly to the left.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\vspace{-2mm}
This study presented a reproducible evaluation of a DRL-based scheduler for mixed-criticality WSNs. Our results confirm that a policy-guided Double-DQN agent can learn a stable and balanced scheduling strategy. While it does not surpass the raw HP-latency of a greedy Strict Priority baseline, it provides a significant **30.5\% reduction in normal-priority telemetry delay**, mitigating queue starvation while maintaining a high **95.78\% delivery rate for critical packets**. This work provides a transparent, statistically grounded benchmark and highlights the necessity of soft reward shaping for developing practical, learning-based controllers in smart energy systems. Future work should explore constrained RL formulations and test for generalization across varied traffic loads.
